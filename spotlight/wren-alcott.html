Wren Alcott

The Salt-Wife’s Daughter · self-published, 2025

“I wrote it for the version of me who couldn’t find it on a shelf.”

Wren Alcott, on why she stopped querying

What is it about?

A lighthouse keeper’s daughter marries the sea to settle her father’s debt, and spends the next four hundred pages negotiating the terms. It’s a romance. It’s also a book about what you owe the people who raised you badly.

Why self-publish it?

Eleven agents told me the same thing in different words: they loved it, they didn’t know the shelf it went on. I got tired of waiting for someone to invent the shelf. So I built one.

What was the hardest part?

Not the writing. The part after, where you finish something good and discover that finishing it was the easy half. Nobody warns you that you’ll need to become a marketing department.

What do you want people to do after reading this?

Read it, obviously. But mostly: if you’ve got a manuscript in a drawer because eleven people didn’t know its shelf — take it out.
